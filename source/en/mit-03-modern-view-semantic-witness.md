## Modern View of Convex Optimization

::: {.source-item #src-mit-l03-p014-i001 data-source-page="14" data-source-order="1"}
- **Traditional view: Pre 1990s**

  - LPs are solved by simplex method.
  - NLPs are solved by gradient/Newton methods.
  - Convex programs are special cases of NLPs.

::: {.source-figure #src-mit-l03-p014-f001 data-source-page="14" data-figure-disposition="omitted-source-graphic"}
**Source-figure description (source page 14, traditional view).** The left
diagram is an oval labeled **LP**, with **Simplex** beneath it. On the right,
overlapping ovals labeled **CONVEX** and **NLP** are connected by the label
**Duality** below the overlap, with **Gradient/Newton** beneath the NLP side.
The diagram presents convex programs as a special region within nonlinear
programming and places duality between the LP and convex views.
:::
:::

::: {.source-item #src-mit-l03-p014-i002 data-source-page="14" data-source-order="2"}
- **Modern view: Post 1990s**

  - LPs are often solved by nonsimplex/convex methods.
  - Convex problems are often solved by the same methods as LPs.
  - “Key distinction is not Linear-Nonlinear but Convex-Nonconvex”
    (Rockafellar).

::: {.source-figure #src-mit-l03-p014-f002 data-source-page="14" data-figure-disposition="omitted-source-graphic"}
**Source-figure description (source page 14, modern view).** The lower
diagram has overlapping ovals labeled **LP** and **CONVEX**, with **Simplex**
under the LP side. Under the overlap are **Duality**, **Cutting plane**,
**Interior point**, and **Subgradient**. A separate oval labeled **NLP** sits
to the right with **Gradient/Newton** underneath. The overlap emphasizes that
convex and linear programs share important solution methods, while general
nonlinear programming remains separate in this comparison.
:::
