### Content progression

1. Page 50: lecture outline and reading reference.
2. Pages 51-53: definition of a recession direction and recession cone,
   five-part Recession Cone Theorem, and the proof that one infinite feasible
   ray suffices to establish a recession direction for every point of a closed
   convex set.
3. Page 54: lineality space and orthogonal decomposition of a convex set.
4. Pages 55-59: epigraph geometry, common recession cones of nonempty
   sublevel sets, six asymptotic descent cases, recession and constancy spaces,
   a positive-semidefinite quadratic example, and the recession function with
   its slope and calculus formulas.
5. Page 60: feasible points, local and global minimizers, local-to-global
   optimality under convexity, and uniqueness under strict convexity.
6. Pages 61-62: compact-sublevel existence criteria, an extended Weierstrass
   theorem, and the convex existence characterization
   `R_X \cap R_f = {0}`.
7. Page 63: existence for proper sums or maxima when one component has
   infinite recession slope in every nonzero direction; the positive-definite
   quadratic application also yields uniqueness.

## Confirmed printed defects requiring transparent correction

The following defects are confirmed from the page renders. The English
semantic witness must preserve the printed form; a learner-facing correction
must be separately disclosed and bound to the relevant stable identifier.

1. **O015-MIT-SEM-0013, page 55 - reversed monotonicity.** The slide first says it is
   characterizing directions of monotonic decrease, but later says that along
   a horizontal recession direction `f` is monotonically **nondecreasing**.
   From `(d,0) in R_epi(f)`, the correct consequence is
   `f(x + alpha d) <= f(x)` for `alpha >= 0`: `f` is nonincreasing.
2. **O015-MIT-SEM-0014, page 57 - direction-symbol mismatch.** All six panels use `d`, but the
   source bullet says `y` is a direction of recession in panels (a)-(d). The
   learner-facing statement should use `d`.
3. **O015-MIT-SEM-0012, pages 56 and 58-63 - function-type arrow.** Seven type declarations use
   the element-mapping arrow where only a domain-to-codomain arrow is intended.
   Preserve the printed arrow in the witness and normalize it to a right arrow
   in the learner-facing type declarations. Do not alter genuine element maps.
4. **O015-MIT-SEM-0015, page 59 - missing set-builder binder.** The printed line
   `R_f = {(d,0) in R_epi(f)}` does not bind the returned vector. The correct
   form is `R_f = {d | (d,0) in R_epi(f)}`.
5. **O015-MIT-SEM-0016, page 60 - omitted local-radius quantifier.** The local-minimizer
   definition names the ball with radius `epsilon` but does not say **for some
   `epsilon > 0`**. That existential condition is required.
6. **O015-MIT-SEM-0017, page 61 - omitted feasibility condition.** The extended Weierstrass
   statement needs `X intersect dom(f)` nonempty; properness of `f` alone does
   not ensure feasibility over an arbitrary closed `X`. Page 62 states this
   condition explicitly.
7. **O015-MIT-SEM-0018, page 61 - malformed constrained-function shorthand.** The proof refers
   to “level sets of `f intersect X`.” A function cannot be intersected with a
   set. The intended objects are `{x in X | f(x) <= gamma}`, equivalently
   sublevel sets of `f + delta_X`.
8. **O015-MIT-SEM-0019, pages 60-63 - minimum/minima terminology.** The source
   repeatedly calls the optimizing point `x*`, and sets of such points,
   “minimum” or “minima.” The learner-facing edition must distinguish a
   `minimizer` (a point) and a `minimizer set` from the `minimum value`; the
   witness must retain the printed terminology and the edition notice must
   disclose this systematic semantic normalization.

## Expository gaps and edition-level inference

These are not recorded as confirmed source errors. They are connective steps
or qualification risks inferred from the mathematics and should be labeled as
edition explanation if added.

1. On page 53, convexity applies because, for all sufficiently large `k`,
   `bar(x) + d_k` lies on the segment from `bar(x)` to `z_k`; the slide leaves
   that coefficient check implicit.
2. On page 59, the secant-slope formula should explicitly quantify the fixed
   base point as `x in dom(f)`. Page 57 supplies the intended independence from
   the starting point, but page 59 does not repeat the condition.
3. Page 59 prints the sum and supremum calculus without restating the required
   properness/common-domain context. The semantic edition should not present
   those formulas as unrestricted identities without checking their stated
   hypotheses in the cited reading.
4. On page 62, the proof needs the approximating scalars chosen strictly above
   `f*` so every `X intersect V_k` is nonempty; the notation `gamma_k downarrow
   f*` conventionally carries this intent but does not spell it out.
5. The source repeatedly calls `{x | f(x) <= gamma}` a “level set.” The object
   is specifically a sublevel set; this is a terminology precision issue, not
   a change to the mathematics.

## Indonesian terminology and transcription risks

- Use `kerucut resesi`, `arah resesi`, `fungsi resesi`, and a stable rendering
  such as `ruang kelinieran` for *lineality space*; avoid conflating lineality
  with an arbitrary linear subspace.
- Translate `{x | f(x) <= gamma}` as `himpunan sublevel`, not merely
  `himpunan level` or `himpunan aras`.
- Distinguish a point `peminimum lokal/global` from the `nilai minimum`.
- Keep `epigraf`, `interior relatif`, `batas relatif`, `semikontinu bawah`,
  `koersif/koersivitas`, `kompak`, `terbatas`, and `proper` distinct.
- Explain the `+` in the page-54 decomposition as a Minkowski sum.
- Render source primes in quadratic forms as transposes, for example
  `x^top Qx + a^top x + b`; do not leave an ambiguous apostrophe.
- Preserve the difference between positive semidefinite and positive definite
  quadratic functions. Only the latter supplies the strict-convexity
  uniqueness conclusion on page 63.
- `Direction of constancy` needs a consistent term such as `arah kekonstanan`;
  do not translate it as another recession direction without its two-sided
  condition.
